Hamsa Safi v1 Fast

Real-time noise suppression built for Arabic voice. Streaming at 10 ms frames, it delivers higher perceived quality than Krisp, Hush and RNNoise on Arabic speech in every condition we tested (phone and wideband, synthetic and real), and the highest perceived quality on Arabic phone audio of any system tested.

Benchmark

Four conditions, six metrics, 876 noisy clips per system. The noise comes from a public test set (MS-SNSD) that was never used to train Safi. Every system runs in real-time streaming mode at full suppression strength.

Listen

Selected clips from the benchmark. Press play, then switch systems mid-sentence. Playback continues from the same point. Each clip is played at one common gain for every system, so you hear each output at its true level. If a system removes part of the speech, it sounds quieter. Scores shown are PESQ / DNSMOS OVRL for that clip.

Real-world recordings

Synthetic mixtures are controlled; real calls are messy. These are genuine noisy Arabic recordings from Mozilla Common Voice (CC0): people speaking into their own laptops and phones, with real rooms, fans, traffic and echo. Nothing was added. There is no clean reference, so quality is scored with DNSMOS P.835. Shown: the 100 noisiest of 1,500 randomly scanned clips, one per speaker, both as recorded (16 kHz) and passed through a G.711 phone channel.

Methodology

Test data

  • Speech: FLEURS test split, Egyptian Arabic (ar_eg, 250 utterances) and US English (en_us, 188 utterances), filtered with DNSMOS for clean references. FLEURS is not in Safi's training data.
  • Noise: MS-SNSD noise_test: air conditioner, babble, neighbour talking, airport announcements, typing, door slams, copy machine, vacuum cleaner, eating. MS-SNSD is not in Safi's training data.
  • Mixing: SNR −5, 0, 5, 10, 15 dB in equal shares, random talker level −30 to −20 dBFS.
  • Telephone: 300–3400 Hz band-pass, 8 kHz, G.711 μ-law round trip, then fed to every system at 16 kHz.

Systems and settings

  • Hamsa Safi v1 Fast: streaming ONNX runtime, 10 ms frames, full strength.
  • Krisp: SDK 1.4.0, krisp-viva-tel-v2 and krisp-viva-pro-v1, 16 kHz, 10 ms frames, level 100.
  • DeepFilterNet3: official 48 kHz checkpoint, deepfilternet 0.5.6, offline enhance (an advantage for it).
  • RNNoise: reference model via pyrnnoise, 48 kHz.
  • Hush: weya-ai/hush with the stateful ONNX streaming runtime, full strength. Its 0.5 wet/dry default scored lower on wideband audio.
  • Fresh session per clip. No system saw the test clips.

Metrics

  • PESQ (ITU-T P.862): narrowband against the 8 kHz reference on phone sets, wideband otherwise.
  • STOI intelligibility and SI-SDR fidelity, against the clean reference.
  • DNSMOS P.835 (SIG / BAK / OVRL): a MOS predictor that needs no reference, trained on human P.835 ratings.
  • Each system's algorithmic delay is compensated by cross-correlation before intrusive scoring.

Reading the numbers

  • On Arabic wideband, Safi is best or tied-best on all six metrics against every system.
  • On phone audio, Safi delivers the highest perceived quality (DNSMOS) in both languages. Krisp Tel v2 keeps a small lead on reference-based PESQ/STOI there (Arabic PESQ 2.60 vs 2.53), mostly on clips where the noise is other people talking.
  • Paired-bootstrap 95% intervals for the headline gaps are in results/.

Deployment

CPU time per 10 ms frame, single thread, AMD EPYC 7R32 (AWS c5a/m5a class). Delay is measured by cross-correlation on the benchmark. DeepFilterNet3 ran offline in Python, so it has no streaming number.
